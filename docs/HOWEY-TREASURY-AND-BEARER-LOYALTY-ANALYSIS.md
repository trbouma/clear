# Howey, Treasury Responsibility, and Bearer Loyalty Units

Status: Legal-policy analysis for program design, not a legal opinion

Research date: October 8, 2026

## Executive assessment

**Clear determines how an instrument is issued and spent. Its issuer determines what the instrument promises and how it is offered. Securities analysis concerns the actual arrangement, not merely its technical representation.**

An individual treasurer should understand these considerations before authorizing a Clear Mint Unit (CMU). However, legal classification should be an issuer-level responsibility supported by qualified counsel, not a decision left to each treasurer acting alone. Delegated signing authority is not delegated immunity from law, nor does holding that role alone establish personal liability.

The purchase-earned loyalty model considered here has a plausible consumptive character: customers receive transferable credits after buying products or services and can redeem them only for the issuing provider's products or services. A later holder may redeem without being the original customer. This resembles a portable promotional entitlement more than an investment when the actual economics and communications support that purpose.

That is a preliminary assessment, not an exemption. Purchase-linked consideration, promotion of resale gains, holding rewards, fundraising, and changes in redemption rights can alter the analysis. Bearer possession neither makes an instrument a security nor prevents it from being one.

## 1. The legal question and source hierarchy

The Supreme Court's decision in *SEC v. W. J. Howey Co.*, 328 U.S. 293 (1946), addresses investment contracts, one category within federal securities law. It examines the economic arrangement rather than requiring a particular certificate, technology, or formal contract. The case concerned citrus property sold with arrangements for others to manage it for purchasers' profit. [Supreme Court decision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/328/293).

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) summarizes the test as an investment of money in a common enterprise with a reasonable expectation of profits derived from others' essential managerial efforts. The elements are sometimes grouped into three rather than four parts; that is a presentation difference, not a different test. [SEC overview](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/transactions-involving-crypto-assets).

The analysis must distinguish statutes and judicial precedent from Commission interpretations, staff guidance, and fact-specific no-action responses. None of the latter supplies a general approval for Clear. Failure to constitute an investment contract also does not exclude other security categories, such as stock or certain notes.

This note focuses on United States federal investment-contract considerations. It does not resolve state securities law, territorial application, Canadian law, or another jurisdiction's rules. The relevant jurisdictions follow the actual program and participants, not merely the server location.

## 2. Current crypto guidance: useful but limited

The SEC marks its 2019 digital-asset framework as withdrawn and superseded by its March 17, 2026 interpretation. It should not be presented as current guidance. [Framework status](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-corporation-finance/framework-investment-contract-analysis-digital-assets).

The 2026 interpretation defines crypto assets by reference to a cryptographically secured distributed ledger. Ordinary Clear bearer proofs are not recorded that way. Its categories therefore should not simply be assigned to CMUs. Its discussion of practical-use instruments is informative, but general securities law remains the starting point. Its airdrop discussion also distinguishes consideration bargained for in exchange for an asset from earlier consideration not provided in that exchange. [Commission interpretation, footnote 1, section III.C, and section VII](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2026/33-11412.pdf).

September 2026 staff guidance discusses promotional representations and current utility, but expressly disclaims independent legal force. Counsel should verify current authorities at launch and upon material changes. A changing enforcement interpretation is not a substitute for reviewing the actual arrangement. [SEC staff FAQs](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-corporation-finance/faqs-crypto-assets).

## 3. Applying each element

### Investment of money or other value

Do not assume that no separate token price means no relevant consideration. Review the entire exchange: what the customer pays, what was advertised, what the customer receives, and whether the reward induced the purchase. Required promotional activity or other services can also complicate a supposedly free distribution.

For loyalty rewards, distinguish a previously announced purchase incentive from a genuinely unexpected later gift to past customers. Preserve evidence of announcement dates, pricing, and conditions. The organization should not rely solely on describing credits as a bonus or allocating their invoice price to zero.

Even if counsel considers this element present, the other elements still matter. Paying for a product accompanied by an ordinary consumptive benefit does not automatically establish an investment contract.

### Common enterprise

Ask whether participants are entering an economic venture with linked fortunes, not simply whether everyone uses one mint. Courts differ in their approaches to commonality; governing precedent requires jurisdiction-specific advice.

A common issuer, pooled operational budget, or common risk of service failure should not be treated as a conclusive answer. More investment-oriented facts would include pooling contributions to generate returns distributed to holders. Document whether holders have any interest in business earnings, assets, or investment proceeds, as distinct from an entitlement to goods or services.

### Reasonable expectation of profits

This is often the central distinction for a loyalty program. In *United Housing Foundation, Inc. v. Forman*, 421 U.S. 837 (1975), the Court distinguished acquisition for use or consumption from investment for profits. The housing arrangement's economic character, not the use of the word stock, controlled. [Supreme Court decision, especially pages 852-858](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/421/837).

A reward redeemable for a product discount or service is not automatically an investment return merely because it has economic value. Conversely, useful redemption does not neutralize an accompanying promise of appreciation or profit sharing. Examine why a reasonable recipient would acquire and retain units, using actual terms and communications rather than a disclaimer alone.

Evidence to review includes unit quantities relative to plausible consumption, scarcity messaging, resale promotion, repurchase arrangements, holding incentives, and promised business expansion. These are investigation prompts, not independent legal elements or numerical safe harbors.

### Essential managerial efforts of others

A merchant must operate its business to honor rewards, just as a service provider must perform a prepaid service. That operational dependence is not, without the rest of the test, a securities conclusion. The relevant connection is between others' managerial efforts and the profits participants reasonably expect.

For Clear, distinguish the mint operator's proof processing, the treasurer's issuance authorization, and the issuer's business promises. A program promoted as benefiting when management expands demand or creates profitable trading opportunities is different from an existing merchant promising a specified redemption benefit.

## 4. The proposed bearer loyalty program

For this scenario, assume:

1. An existing provider sells ordinary products or services.
2. Eligible purchases earn units under disclosed promotional terms.
3. The units can be held, given away, pooled, or transferred to another person.
4. Only the provider accepts them for its own specified goods or services.
5. They carry no equity, profit distribution, interest, or promised cash return.
6. Redemption does not require the holder to be the original purchaser, subject to applicable eligibility and legal requirements.

These are analytical assumptions to confirm, not features guaranteed by Clear software.

### Illustrative example

A provider awards ten credits after a qualifying service purchase. Each credit gives a defined reduction in the price of a later eligible service. The customer gives the credits to a friend, who presents valid proofs and receives that reduction. The provider retires the accepted proofs.

The friend is exercising a service entitlement, not necessarily investing in the provider. Portability changes who can obtain the benefit; it does not inherently change the benefit into a share of business success. That is the strongest rationale for this design.

The analysis changes if the provider says that limited supply will make credits appreciate as it grows, offers yield for retaining them, or solicits large purchases to fund a future platform while promoting resale gains. Those facts require renewed review even if credits remain redeemable for services.

### Preliminary conclusion

Under the stated assumptions, the better starting characterization is a transferable loyalty or redemption entitlement with a consumptive purpose. The strongest argument is the actual service benefit and absence of a profit-oriented arrangement, not a categorical assertion that nobody invested money.

Only-provider redemption narrows utility but is not itself a securities exemption. Likewise, calling the program closed-loop is a commercial description unless a particular statute's definition and conditions have been examined.

## 5. Transferability and later purchasers

Giving away a reward, selling it at a discount to someone who wants the service, and marketing it as an appreciating asset are economically different activities. Transferability is relevant evidence, not a switch that alone determines security status.

An informal secondary market can emerge without the issuer creating one. The issuer should review what it facilitates, promises, or endorses. A new platform or promoter could package units into a separate investment arrangement; classification of the original reward does not conclusively resolve every later transaction.

In the 2019 *TurnKey Jet* no-action response, SEC staff emphasized an operational service, immediate usability, fixed-value terms, restricted transfers, and consumption-focused marketing. The response was fact-specific, not a generally available exemption. Unrestricted Clear wallet-to-wallet circulation differs materially from that program's platform-wallet restriction. [SEC staff response](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/corpfin/cf-noaction/2019/turnkey-jet-040219-2a1.htm).

This comparison is useful precisely because it exposes the difference; it should not be cited as SEC approval of anonymous transferable loyalty proofs.

## 6. What bearer redemption changes

The provider can design acceptance around the instrument rather than the original customer's identity. That may reduce the need for individual loyalty balance accounts and allow recipients who never made the qualifying purchase to enjoy the benefit.

However, distinguish four questions: possession of proof material, signature authenticity, unspent status, and entitlement to the promised redemption. A mint status query alone does not establish all four. Recipients normally swap transferred proofs for fresh ones; redemption must invalidate accepted inputs consistently. Copied proofs are not final offline settlement.

"Without regard to who uses them" should mean **the benefit is not restricted to the original purchaser**, not a promise to ignore legally relevant facts. Restricted goods, geography, sanctions, fraud controls, or other applicable requirements may affect acceptance. Whether any identification is required needs separate review; neither blanket identification nor blanket anonymity follows from Howey.

Blind signatures also do not conceal a shipping address, an identified service appointment, or information in a support request. The provider should accurately describe payment privacy rather than promise complete transaction anonymity.

### Returns and chargebacks

A particularly important design conflict arises when the original purchase is reversed after rewards have circulated. The provider may be unable to associate fresh bearer proofs with that purchase or reclaim them from innocent later holders.

Possible policies include delayed reward issuance until a return period closes, a disclosed and lawful adjustment to the purchaser's refund, or treating issued rewards as an accepted promotional cost. Each option needs consumer-law and operational review. Do not promise targeted cancellation of an original reward after unlinkable swaps unless the system actually supports it.

### Expiry, changes, and disputes

If expiry or different redemption conditions apply, the program must preserve those conditions through swaps and wallet presentation. Do not assume an arbitrary policy date is automatically enforced by existing Cashu proofs. Any cohort or unit design requires technical validation and consideration of fragmentation and privacy effects.

Later holders need accessible terms even if they never saw the original promotion. Publish authoritative unit identity, benefit, exclusions, acceptance locations, and change rules. Provide a dispute process that does not ask customers to disclose live reusable proof secrets in ordinary support channels.

## 7. Responsibility belongs to the issuer, with a treasurer review gate

| Role | Recommended responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Issuer or program sponsor | Define obligations, approve program economics and communications, obtain legal review |
| Treasurer | Issue only within approved unit policy and limits; escalate departures |
| Mint operator | Implement approved controls, preserve spent state, report incidents and actual capabilities |
| Product and marketing owners | Keep offers, wallet descriptions, and campaigns consistent with approved terms |
| Legal advisers | Assess classification, jurisdictions, distribution, redemption, and material changes |

This is a governance recommendation, not a determination of statutory responsibility or personal liability. A person's actual conduct and role matter more than an internal title.

Each exact CMU should have a review record. Several units sharing one operator do not share one legal conclusion. The same technical unit used in materially different offerings may also need separate analysis.

The approval record should include the following:

- Issuer identity, jurisdictions, responsible decision-makers, and policy version.
- The precise entitlement and what the holder does not receive.
- How units are earned or purchased, including purchase-linked consideration.
- Immediate service availability, issuance limits, and expected redemption capacity.
- Transfer, resale, refund, expiry, and program-termination arrangements.
- Public communications and any promoter or marketplace relationships.
- Element-by-element Howey reasoning, assumptions, unresolved questions, and counsel's review scope.
- Other regulatory review and triggers for reassessment.

An internal review status must not be displayed as SEC approval. Clear can support evidence and governance workflows without purporting to automate legal classification.

## 8. Changes requiring renewed review

| Change | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Introduce yield or revenue participation | Changes the benefit toward investment returns |
| Promote scarcity or future resale gains | May change reasonable recipient expectations |
| Sell large advance allocations for a future service | Changes funding purpose and dependence on future development |
| Add cash repurchases or exchange support | Changes exit economics and potentially other regulatory analysis |
| Add unrelated accepting providers | Changes the service boundary and settlement relationships |
| Expand countries or user categories | Changes applicable law and obligations |
| Alter redemption, expiry, or returns policy | Changes holder rights and the original analytical assumptions |

These are review triggers, not assertions that each change creates a security. If counsel concludes an offering involves securities, registration or an available exemption and associated obligations must be addressed before proceeding; relabeling the units is not a remedy.

## 9. Beyond Howey

A non-security conclusion does not settle gift-card, prepaid-access, money-transmission, consumer-protection, tax, accounting, privacy, sanctions, or unclaimed-property questions. These are separate issue areas, not a claim that every regime necessarily applies.

For example, federal Regulation E includes specific treatment for loyalty, award, and promotional gift cards, including conditions and disclosures. Whether a particular bearer program fits requires reviewing its actual form and terms; the word loyalty is not sufficient. State rules may differ. [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, 12 CFR 1005.20](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/).

Counsel should separately assess whether transferability, redemption scope, cash conversion, or program size affects any relevant exemption. Accounting advisers should determine how earned units and expected redemption are recorded. Privacy advisers should assess whether retained purchase and redemption metadata undermines the program's promises.

## 10. Recommended position for Clear

Clear can support **issuer-accountable, holder-portable service entitlements**. The loyalty scenario is a useful example because issuing authority and redemption responsibility remain with the provider while the benefit can circulate beyond the original customer.

The appropriate design discipline is to define the genuine consumptive purpose, document the full economic arrangement, align technical behavior and communications with those terms, and review changes. It is not to engineer a vocabulary that appears to evade the test.

For treasurers, the practical rule is: **authorize issuance only under an approved program, and escalate changes to what units promise, how they are distributed, or how holders are encouraged to use them.** For issuers, the corresponding obligation is to supply that review and maintain credible redemption.
