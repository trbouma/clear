# Community First, Circulating Credits

**A community should be able to make its resources easier to share without
first turning its relationships into an investment market.** Clear offers a
possible way to issue private, portable credits recognized by participating
providers, while leaving the community responsible for what those credits mean.

The starting point is an agreed service or benefit, not a speculative asset,
a universal payment network, or a promise to maintain a currency peg. The
policy opportunity is greater holder agency with accountable issuance and
redemption. This is a proposed use of Clear, not a claim of regulatory exemption
or production readiness.

## Begin with acceptance

A community-first program begins with practical questions: what can people
obtain, who undertakes to provide it, and who may authorize allocations?

For example, a community workshop could allocate credits redeemable for
specified equipment sessions. A holder unable to attend could give credits to
another person. The workshop would accept valid notes under published terms,
without necessarily requiring the new holder to maintain a billing balance.
Safety training or booking requirements could remain separate from payment.

The credit makes an existing entitlement portable. It need not promise an
investment return, cash conversion, or appreciation as the workshop grows.
Where several providers accept a unit, their capacity commitments and any
reimbursement arrangements must be explicit. Shared recognition does not
automatically create a shared guarantee.

**An acceptance boundary is not a circulation boundary.** Unrestricted bearer
notes can pass to outsiders even when only community providers redeem them.
A program that limits benefits to members must address eligibility separately,
with consequences for privacy and transferability.

## Accounting without a holder ledger

An account-credit system ordinarily records each account's balance and updates
those records when allocations move. It can offer transfers and pseudonymous
accounts, but redistribution generally remains an operator-mediated account
operation.

Clear lets wallets hold bearer Mint Notes. The issuer can account for authorized
issuance and redemption without necessarily maintaining a named balance for
every holder or a complete history of intermediate exchanges. Users can share,
pool, or delegate allocations instead of repeatedly requesting administrative
reassignment.

This is **cash-like privacy, not the absence of accounting**. The mint maintains
authoritative pending and spent state; it sees amounts involved in its
operations. Blind signatures help separate issuance from later spending, while
metadata, service logins, and physical delivery can reveal identities. Recipients
normally swap incoming proofs for fresh ones before relying on them. This is
not final offline settlement.

Holder agency also has costs. Lost or stolen notes may not be recoverable,
and a community cannot assume it can revoke a particular person's allocation
after unrestricted transfers. Named accounts may remain preferable for
non-transferable benefits or programs requiring individualized recovery.

## Avoid unnecessary financialization

Here, financialization means adding investment, trading, or financial-return
features to a program whose purpose is access to services. It does not mean
that all payments, prices, or accounting are undesirable.

| Community need | Additional feature that need not be assumed |
| --- | --- |
| Share an unused service entitlement | A speculative secondary market |
| Keep allocations portable | An exchange listing or universal convertibility |
| Define a service benefit | A promise of dollar redemption at par |
| Pay for reliable mint operation | Operator ownership of units or income from reserves |
| Demonstrate responsible issuance | A named record of every holder's spending |

Clear can support an issuer-defined Clear Mint Unit (CMU) without a mandatory
blockchain settlement layer or external-currency peg. A service entitlement
still creates a fulfillment obligation: the community must provide the service
it promises. Choosing cash redemption or broader convertibility adds obligations
that must be assessed on their own merits.

This is not a claim that communities must build mining networks to use
stablecoins. Blockchain users normally rely on existing infrastructure, and
not all networks use mining. Nor are stablecoin issuers and technical operators
necessarily the same party. Clear's practical option is to operate a mint, or
contract for one, without requiring that infrastructure or those economic
features for the community's own unit.

## Separate service operation from program authority

The community can define the entitlement, delegate issuance to a treasurer,
and hire an operator to run the mint. The operator could earn an agreed service
fee without owning circulating units or benefiting from reserve income.

That separation needs enforceable arrangements and technical controls. An
operator still has consequential responsibilities for signing, availability,
and spent-state integrity. Contracts should address access to records, backups,
continuity, incident handling, and an orderly operator transition. Simple
deployment does not mean effortless or risk-free operation.

This division allows a community to obtain technical expertise without handing
over the authority to redefine its program. It also makes responsibility
visible: a software fault, unauthorized issuance, and failure to deliver a
promised service are different failures requiring different responses.

## Proportionate safeguards, not assumed exemptions

The policy case is for obligations proportionate to actual activities and risks,
not for declaring community transactions outside the law. The same label can
cover very different programs. Scale, funding, cash conversion, eligible goods,
transfer arrangements, and jurisdictions matter.

Canada's 2025 national risk assessment explicitly supports risk-based measures
that consider privacy, financial integrity, and regulatory burden. It also
distinguishes the lower inherent vulnerability of closed-loop prepaid products
from open-loop products. That is useful policy context, not a classification
of Clear or a finding that every community program is low risk.
[Department of Finance Canada: 2025 risk assessment](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/nira-neri/2025/report.html).

Restricted acceptance does not eliminate money laundering or require a
complicit treasurer for misuse to occur. Resale, merchant misconduct, and
redemption into readily resold goods remain relevant. Accountable providers,
reviewed issuance, reconciliation, and appropriate redemption controls can
address risks, but the sufficiency of those controls requires program-specific
assessment. Private circulation is not permission to disregard applicable
identification or reporting requirements.

Likewise, avoiding investment features does not remove consumer obligations.
For example, United States gift-card rules address electronic codes and
promises as well as physical cards, with distinctions and exclusions based on
their actual terms. Other jurisdictions require their own review.
[Consumer Financial Protection Bureau: Regulation E, section 1005.20](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/).

The appropriate approach is to review a genuine service program as such,
without adding unnecessary financial features and without disguising financial
activity through different terminology.

## A community decision before a technology decision

Before introducing circulating credits, a community should agree on:

- The exact benefit, responsible issuer, and providers willing to accept it.
- Who receives allocations and whether onward transfer is desirable.
- Capacity and issuance limits, service changes, and program closure terms.
- What records are necessary, who controls them, and what privacy can be promised.
- Accessible redemption, wallet-loss support, and arrangements during outages.
- Applicable legal obligations and changes that would trigger renewed review.

The community should also be free to conclude that an ordinary voucher or
account system better serves its needs. Clear is advantageous where portable
possession and private circulation solve a real coordination problem.

**The aim is community agency without unnecessary financial complexity:
accountable institutions, understandable entitlements, and holders who can
exercise meaningful choice over how their allocations are used.**
